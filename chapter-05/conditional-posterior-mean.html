Conditional posterior of θ given σ² (Section 5.2)

Model. $$Y_i\mid\theta,\sigma^2\overset{iid}{\sim}N(\theta,\sigma^2),\qquad \sigma^2\text{ known},\qquad \theta\mid\sigma^2\sim N(\mu_0,\tau_0^2).$$

Proof. By Bayes' rule, keeping only factors that involve \(\theta\),

$$p(\theta\mid y_1,\dots,y_n,\sigma^2)\;\propto\;p(\theta\mid\sigma^2)\,p(y_1,\dots,y_n\mid\theta,\sigma^2). \tag{1}$$

The likelihood is

$$p(y_1,\dots,y_n\mid\theta,\sigma^2)=(2\pi\sigma^2)^{-n/2}\exp\Big\{-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\theta)^2\Big\}\;\propto\;\exp\Big\{-\frac1{2\sigma^2}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\theta)^2\Big\}. \tag{2}$$

The prior is

$$p(\theta\mid\sigma^2)\;\propto\;\exp\Big\{-\frac1{2\tau_0^2}(\theta-\mu_0)^2\Big\}. \tag{3}$$

Substituting (2) and (3) into (1) and adding exponents,

$$p(\theta\mid y_1,\dots,y_n,\sigma^2)\;\propto\;\exp\Big\{-\frac12\Big[\frac{(\theta-\mu_0)^2}{\tau_0^2}+\frac{\sum_i(y_i-\theta)^2}{\sigma^2}\Big]\Big\}. \tag{4}$$

Expand the sum of squares:

$$\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\theta)^2=\sum_i y_i^2-2\theta\sum_i y_i+n\theta^2. \tag{5}$$

Insert (5) into the bracket of (4) and collect powers of \(\theta\):

$$\frac{\theta^2-2\theta\mu_0+\mu_0^2}{\tau_0^2}+\frac{\sum_i y_i^2-2\theta\sum_i y_i+n\theta^2}{\sigma^2}=a\theta^2-2b\theta+c, \tag{6}$$

where

$$a=\frac1{\tau_0^2}+\frac n{\sigma^2},\qquad b=\frac{\mu_0}{\tau_0^2}+\frac{\sum_i y_i}{\sigma^2},\qquad c=\frac{\mu_0^2}{\tau_0^2}+\frac{\sum_i y_i^2}{\sigma^2}.$$

The term \(c\) does not involve \(\theta\), so

$$p(\theta\mid y_1,\dots,y_n,\sigma^2)\;\propto\;\exp\Big\{-\frac12\big(a\theta^2-2b\theta\big)\Big\}. \tag{7}$$

Complete the square:

$$a\theta^2-2b\theta=a\Big(\theta-\frac ba\Big)^2-\frac{b^2}{a}. \tag{8}$$

The term \(b^2/a\) does not involve \(\theta\), so

$$p(\theta\mid y_1,\dots,y_n,\sigma^2)\;\propto\;\exp\Big\{-\frac12\,a\Big(\theta-\frac ba\Big)^2\Big\}=\exp\Big\{-\frac12\Big(\frac{\theta-b/a}{1/\sqrt a}\Big)^2\Big\}. \tag{9}$$

Expression (9) is the kernel of a normal density with mean \(b/a\) and variance \(1/a\).

Set \(\tau_n^2=1/a\) and \(\mu_n=b/a\). Using \(\sum_i y_i=n\bar y\),

$$\tau_n^2=\frac{1}{\frac1{\tau_0^2}+\frac n{\sigma^2}},\qquad \mu_n=\frac{\frac1{\tau_0^2}\mu_0+\frac n{\sigma^2}\bar y}{\frac1{\tau_0^2}+\frac n{\sigma^2}}.$$

Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\theta\mid y_1,\dots,y_n,\sigma^2\;\sim\;N(\mu_n,\tau_n^2)\;}\qquad\blacksquare$$

Dropping constants

Any factor that does not involve \(\theta\) multiplies the posterior by a constant. The normalizing constant \(1/p(y_1,\dots,y_n\mid\sigma^2)\) and \((2\pi\sigma^2)^{-n/2}\) are of this type, so they are absorbed into the proportionality sign.

The expansion

$$(y_i-\theta)^2=y_i^2-2\theta y_i+\theta^2.$$

Summing over \(i=1,\dots,n\) gives \(\sum y_i^2-2\theta\sum y_i+n\theta^2\), since \(\theta^2\) is added \(n\) times.

Completing the square

Expand the right side of (8):

$$a\Big(\theta-\frac ba\Big)^2-\frac{b^2}{a}=a\theta^2-2b\theta+\frac{b^2}{a}-\frac{b^2}{a}=a\theta^2-2b\theta.$$

Then \(\exp\{-\frac12(a\theta^2-2b\theta)\}=\exp\{-\frac12 a(\theta-b/a)^2\}\exp\{\frac{b^2}{2a}\}\), and the last factor is constant in \(\theta\).

Kernel determines the density

The \(N(m,v)\) density is \((2\pi v)^{-1/2}\exp\{-\frac12((\theta-m)/\sqrt v)^2\}\). In (9), \(m=b/a\) and \(v=1/a\). Two densities with proportional kernels are equal after normalization, so the posterior is \(N(b/a,1/a)\).
