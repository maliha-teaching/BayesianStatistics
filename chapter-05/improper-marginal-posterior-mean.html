Marginal posterior of θ (Section 5.3)

We want the marginal posterior of \(\theta\). We integrate \(\sigma^2\) out of the joint posterior and recognize a Student \(t\) kernel.

Model. $$Y_i\mid\theta,\sigma^2\overset{iid}{\sim}N(\theta,\sigma^2),\qquad p(\theta,\sigma^2)\propto\frac{1}{\sigma^2}.$$

Proof. With \(\bar y=\frac1n\sum_i y_i\) and \(s^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2\), write

$$A(\theta)=(n-1)s^2+n(\theta-\bar y)^2 .$$

The joint posterior from the conditional derivation is

$$p(\theta,\sigma^2\mid y)\;\propto\;(\sigma^2)^{-(n/2+1)}\exp\Big\{-\frac{A(\theta)}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}. \tag{1}$$

Integrate over \(\sigma^2\):

$$p(\theta\mid y)\;\propto\;\int_0^\infty(\sigma^2)^{-(n/2+1)}\exp\Big\{-\frac{A(\theta)}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}\,d\sigma^2. \tag{2}$$

The integrand is an inverse-gamma kernel in \(\sigma^2\) with shape \(\frac n2\) and scale \(\frac{A(\theta)}2\). Its integral is \(\Gamma(\frac n2)\big(\frac{A(\theta)}2\big)^{-n/2}\).

Only \(A(\theta)\) involves \(\theta\), so \(\Gamma(\frac n2)\) and \(2^{n/2}\) are constants and

$$p(\theta\mid y)\;\propto\;A(\theta)^{-n/2}. \tag{3}$$

Factor \((n-1)s^2\) out of \(A(\theta)\):

$$p(\theta\mid y)\;\propto\;\Big[1+\frac{n(\theta-\bar y)^2}{(n-1)s^2}\Big]^{-n/2}. \tag{4}$$

Let \(t=\dfrac{\sqrt n\,(\theta-\bar y)}{s}\). Then \(\dfrac{n(\theta-\bar y)^2}{(n-1)s^2}=\dfrac{t^2}{n-1}\), so

$$p(\theta\mid y)\;\propto\;\Big[1+\frac{t^2}{n-1}\Big]^{-n/2}. \tag{5}$$

The \(t_\nu\) kernel is \([1+t^2/\nu]^{-(\nu+1)/2}\). With \(\nu=n-1\), \((\nu+1)/2=n/2\), which matches (5). Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\frac{\theta-\bar y}{s/\sqrt n}\;\Big|\;y\;\sim\;t_{n-1}\;}$$

Equivalently, \(\theta\mid y\) is a location–scale \(t\) with location \(\bar y\), scale \(s/\sqrt n\) and \(n-1\) degrees of freedom. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

The inverse-gamma integral

For \(a,b>0\),

$$\int_0^\infty x^{-(a+1)}e^{-b/x}\,dx=\Gamma(a)\,b^{-a}.$$

Let \(u=1/x\). Then \(x=u^{-1}\), \(dx=-u^{-2}du\), and the limits swap, which cancels the minus sign:

$$\int_0^\infty u^{a+1}e^{-bu}\,u^{-2}\,du=\int_0^\infty u^{a-1}e^{-bu}\,du=\frac{\Gamma(a)}{b^{a}}.$$

In (2), \(a=\frac n2\) and \(b=\frac{A(\theta)}2\).

Factoring out the constant

Write

$$A(\theta)=(n-1)s^2\Big[1+\frac{n(\theta-\bar y)^2}{(n-1)s^2}\Big].$$

Then \(A(\theta)^{-n/2}=\big((n-1)s^2\big)^{-n/2}\Big[1+\dots\Big]^{-n/2}\). The first factor does not involve \(\theta\), so it is absorbed into the constant.

The change of variable to t

The map \(\theta\mapsto t\) is linear: \(\theta=\bar y+st/\sqrt n\) and \(d\theta=(s/\sqrt n)\,dt\). The Jacobian does not depend on \(t\), so it only changes the normalizing constant and the kernel in (5) is unchanged.

A density is determined by its kernel, so \(t\mid y\) has the \(t_{n-1}\) density.
