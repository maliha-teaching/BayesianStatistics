Marginal posterior of 1/σ² (Section 5.3)

We want the posterior of the precision \(\tilde\sigma^2=1/\sigma^2\). We integrate \(\theta\) out of the joint posterior and recognize a Gamma kernel.

Model. $$Y_i\mid\theta,\sigma^2\overset{iid}{\sim}N(\theta,\sigma^2),\qquad p(\theta,\sigma^2)\propto\frac{1}{\sigma^2}.$$

Proof. With \(\bar y=\frac1n\sum_i y_i\) and \(s^2=\frac1{n-1}\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2\), the joint posterior from the conditional derivation is

$$p(\theta,\sigma^2\mid y)\;\propto\;(\sigma^2)^{-(n/2+1)}\exp\Big\{-\frac{(n-1)s^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}\exp\Big\{-\frac{n(\theta-\bar y)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}. \tag{1}$$

Integrate over \(\theta\). The first two factors do not involve \(\theta\), so they come out of the integral:

$$p(\sigma^2\mid y)\;\propto\;(\sigma^2)^{-(n/2+1)}\exp\Big\{-\frac{(n-1)s^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\exp\Big\{-\frac{n(\theta-\bar y)^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}d\theta. \tag{2}$$

The integral is the unnormalized \(N(\bar y,\sigma^2/n)\) density, so it equals \(\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2/n}\propto(\sigma^2)^{1/2}\).

The powers of \(\sigma^2\) combine as \(-(\frac n2+1)+\frac12=-(\frac{n-1}2+1)\), so

$$p(\sigma^2\mid y)\;\propto\;(\sigma^2)^{-\left(\frac{n-1}2+1\right)}\exp\Big\{-\frac{(n-1)s^2}{2\sigma^2}\Big\}. \tag{3}$$

This is an inverse-gamma kernel with shape \(\frac{n-1}2\) and scale \(\frac{(n-1)s^2}2\).

Now change variables to \(\tilde\sigma^2=1/\sigma^2\). Then \(\sigma^2=(\tilde\sigma^2)^{-1}\) and the Jacobian is \(\left|\frac{d\sigma^2}{d\tilde\sigma^2}\right|=(\tilde\sigma^2)^{-2}\). Substituting into (3):

$$p(\tilde\sigma^2\mid y)\;\propto\;(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{n-1}2+1}\,e^{-\frac{(n-1)s^2}{2}\tilde\sigma^2}\cdot(\tilde\sigma^2)^{-2}=(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{n-1}2-1}\,e^{-\frac{(n-1)s^2}{2}\tilde\sigma^2}. \tag{4}$$

This is a Gamma kernel. Since \((n-1)s^2=\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2\),

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\sigma^2\mid y\;\sim\;\text{Gamma}\!\Big(\frac{n-1}{2},\ \frac{(n-1)s^2}{2}\Big)\quad(\text{shape, rate})\;}$$

Equivalently, \(\sigma^2\mid y\) is inverse-gamma with the same parameters. \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

Gaussian integral

For any \(m\) and \(v>0\),

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-(\theta-m)^2/(2v)}\,d\theta=\sqrt{2\pi v}.$$

Here \(m=\bar y\) and \(v=\sigma^2/n\), so the integral is \(\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2/n}\). This now depends on \(\sigma^2\), so only the constants \(\sqrt{2\pi/n}\) may be dropped, and \((\sigma^2)^{1/2}\) must be kept.

Gamma and inverse-gamma kernels

Gamma\((a,b)\) with rate \(b\), and inverse-gamma\((a,b)\) with scale \(b\), have kernels

$$\phi^{a-1}e^{-b\phi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad x^{-(a+1)}e^{-b/x}.$$

Matching (3) gives \(a=\frac{n-1}2\) and \(b=\frac{(n-1)s^2}2\). If \(X\) is inverse-gamma\((a,b)\), then \(1/X\) is Gamma\((a,b)\), which is what (4) shows.

The Jacobian

If \(\phi=1/x\), then \(x=1/\phi\) and \(p_\phi(\phi)=p_x(1/\phi)\,\left|\frac{dx}{d\phi}\right|=p_x(1/\phi)\,\phi^{-2}\).

In (3), \((1/\phi)^{-(a+1)}=\phi^{a+1}\) and \(e^{-b/(1/\phi)}=e^{-b\phi}\). Multiplying by \(\phi^{-2}\) gives \(\phi^{a-1}e^{-b\phi}\).
