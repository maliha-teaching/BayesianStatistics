Marginal posterior of the variance (Section 5.3)

We work with the precision \(\tilde\sigma^2=1/\sigma^2\), because the gamma family is conjugate for it (equivalently, \(\sigma^2\) has an inverse-gamma distribution). From here on we write \(\tilde\sigma^2\).

Model. $$Y_i\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2\overset{iid}{\sim}N\!\Big(\theta,\frac{1}{\tilde\sigma^2}\Big),\quad \theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2\sim N\!\Big(\mu_0,\frac{1}{k_0\tilde\sigma^2}\Big),\quad \tilde\sigma^2\sim\text{Gamma}\!\Big(\frac{\nu_0}{2},\frac{\nu_0\sigma_0^2}{2}\Big)$$

Proof. The joint posterior is proportional to the likelihood times the joint prior, and the joint prior factors as \(p(\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)=p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\tilde\sigma^2)\). Hence

$$p(\theta,\tilde\sigma^2\mid y)\;\propto\;p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\tilde\sigma^2).$$

To obtain the marginal posterior of \(\tilde\sigma^2\), integrate out \(\theta\). Since \(p(\tilde\sigma^2)\) does not involve \(\theta\), it moves outside the integral:

$$p(\tilde\sigma^2\mid y)=\int p(\theta,\tilde\sigma^2\mid y)\,d\theta\;\propto\;p(\tilde\sigma^2)\int p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\,d\theta. \tag{1}$$

The prior of \(\theta\) given \(\tilde\sigma^2\) has precision \(k_0\tilde\sigma^2\), so

$$p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{1/2}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_0}{2}(\theta-\mu_0)^2\Big\}. \tag{2}$$

The likelihood is, with \(s^2=\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\bar y)^2\) the sample variance,

$$p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{n/2}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\sum_{i=1}^n(y_i-\theta)^2\Big\}=(\tilde\sigma^2)^{n/2}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\big[(n-1)s^2+n(\bar y-\theta)^2\big]\Big\}. \tag{3}$$

Multiplying (2) and (3), and writing \(Q(\theta)=n(\bar y-\theta)^2+k_0(\theta-\mu_0)^2\),

$$p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\big[(n-1)s^2+Q(\theta)\big]\Big\}.$$

Now, with \(k_n=k_0+n\) and \(\mu_n=\dfrac{k_0\mu_0+n\bar y}{k_n}\), we have

$$Q(\theta)=k_n(\theta-\mu_n)^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2 .$$

so

$$p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\Big[(n-1)s^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2\Big]\Big\}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\}. \tag{4}$$

The first two factors of (4) are free of \(\theta\) and come outside the integral:

$$\int p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\,d\theta\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\Big[(n-1)s^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2\Big]\Big\}\int\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\}d\theta .$$

The remaining integral is the area of a Normal kernel:

$$\int\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\}d\theta=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}}\;\propto\;(\tilde\sigma^2)^{-1/2}.$$

The powers of \(\tilde\sigma^2\) combine as \((\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}(\tilde\sigma^2)^{-\frac12}=(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac n2}\), so

$$\int p(y\mid\theta,\tilde\sigma^2)\,p(\theta\mid\tilde\sigma^2)\,d\theta\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{n/2}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\Big[(n-1)s^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2\Big]\Big\}. \tag{5}$$

Now return the prior of \(\tilde\sigma^2\), which is

$$p(\tilde\sigma^2)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{\nu_0}{2}-1}\exp\Big\{-\tilde\sigma^2\frac{\nu_0\sigma_0^2}{2}\Big\}.$$

Substituting this and (5) into (1),

$$p(\tilde\sigma^2\mid y)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{\nu_0}{2}-1}e^{-\tilde\sigma^2\frac{\nu_0\sigma_0^2}{2}}\cdot(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac n2}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2}{2}\Big[(n-1)s^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2\Big]\Big\}.$$

Adding the powers and combining the exponents,

$$p(\tilde\sigma^2\mid y)\propto(\tilde\sigma^2)^{\frac{\nu_0+n}{2}-1}\exp\Big\{-\tilde\sigma^2\,\frac{\nu_0\sigma_0^2+(n-1)s^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2}{2}\Big\}.$$

This is the kernel \(x^{\alpha-1}e^{-\beta x}\) of a gamma density. Therefore

$$\boxed{\;\tilde\sigma^2\mid y\sim\text{Gamma}\!\left(\frac{\nu_0+n}{2},\;\frac{\nu_0\sigma_0^2+(n-1)s^2+\frac{nk_0}{k_0+n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2}{2}\right)\;}$$

With \(\nu_n=\nu_0+n\), this is \(\text{Gamma}(\nu_n/2,\,\nu_n\sigma_n^2/2)\) with

$$\nu_n\sigma_n^2=\nu_0\sigma_0^2+(n-1)s^2+\frac{k_0n}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2. \qquad\blacksquare$$

Split of the sum

$$\sum(y_i-\theta)^2=\sum\big[(y_i-\bar y)+(\bar y-\theta)\big]^2=\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2+2(\bar y-\theta)\sum(y_i-\bar y)+n(\bar y-\theta)^2 .$$

Since \(\sum(y_i-\bar y)=0\), the middle term vanishes. Also \(\sum(y_i-\bar y)^2=(n-1)s^2\), so the sum equals \((n-1)s^2+n(\bar y-\theta)^2\).

Decomposition of \(Q(\theta)\)

Expand each square:

$$n(\bar y-\theta)^2=n\bar y^2-2n\bar y\,\theta+n\theta^2,\qquad k_0(\theta-\mu_0)^2=k_0\theta^2-2k_0\mu_0\,\theta+k_0\mu_0^2 .$$

Add and collect powers of \(\theta\), using \(n+k_0=k_n\):

$$Q(\theta)=k_n\theta^2-2(n\bar y+k_0\mu_0)\theta+n\bar y^2+k_0\mu_0^2 .$$

Factor \(k_n\) out of the \(\theta\) terms and use \(\mu_n=\dfrac{n\bar y+k_0\mu_0}{k_n}\):

$$Q(\theta)=k_n\big[\theta^2-2\mu_n\theta\big]+n\bar y^2+k_0\mu_0^2 .$$

Add and subtract \(\mu_n^2\) inside the bracket:

$$Q(\theta)=k_n(\theta-\mu_n)^2-k_n\mu_n^2+n\bar y^2+k_0\mu_0^2 .$$

Since \(k_n\mu_n^2=\dfrac{(n\bar y+k_0\mu_0)^2}{k_n}\), the \(\theta\)-free terms over \(k_n\) are

$$-k_n\mu_n^2+n\bar y^2+k_0\mu_0^2=\frac{(n+k_0)(n\bar y^2+k_0\mu_0^2)-(n\bar y+k_0\mu_0)^2}{k_n}.$$

Expand the product and the square:

$$(n+k_0)(n\bar y^2+k_0\mu_0^2)=n^2\bar y^2+nk_0\mu_0^2+nk_0\bar y^2+k_0^2\mu_0^2,$$ $$(n\bar y+k_0\mu_0)^2=n^2\bar y^2+2nk_0\bar y\mu_0+k_0^2\mu_0^2 .$$

Subtracting, the terms \(n^2\bar y^2\) and \(k_0^2\mu_0^2\) cancel:

$$nk_0\mu_0^2+nk_0\bar y^2-2nk_0\bar y\mu_0=nk_0(\bar y^2-2\bar y\mu_0+\mu_0^2)=nk_0(\bar y-\mu_0)^2 .$$

Dividing by \(k_n\) gives \(\dfrac{nk_0}{k_n}(\bar y-\mu_0)^2\), which proves the decomposition.

Finally, the exponent in (4) is a sum and \(e^{A+B}=e^Ae^B\), so the only factor containing \(\theta\) is the one with \((\theta-\mu_n)^2\).

Normal integral

The integral we have is

$$\int\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\}d\theta .$$

This is a Normal kernel in \(\theta\) with mean \(\mu_n\) and precision \(\tilde\sigma^2k_n\). The Normal density with these parameters is

$$\sqrt{\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2\pi}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\},$$

which integrates to 1 over \(\theta\). So multiply and divide the integral by its normalising constant:

$$\int\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\}d\theta=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}}\underbrace{\int\sqrt{\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2\pi}}\exp\Big\{-\frac{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}{2}(\theta-\mu_n)^2\Big\}d\theta}_{=\,1}=\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}} .$$

Writing \(\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi}{\tilde\sigma^2k_n}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{2\pi}{k_n}}\,(\tilde\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\), the factor \(\sqrt{2\pi/k_n}\) does not involve \(\tilde\sigma^2\), so it is absorbed in \(\propto\). This leaves \((\tilde\sigma^2)^{-1/2}\), which is the factor used in the main equation.
